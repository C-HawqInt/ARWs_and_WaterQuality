How this was built

What it combines

C-HAWQ's statewide residential canal inventory (canal polygons with county, size, form, connectivity, receiving water and number of mouths, plus navigable centerlines) joined to public water quality monitoring from the national Water Quality Portal. The portal carries Florida Department of Environmental Protection monitoring, county and city programs, Florida LAKEWATCH, the U.S. Geological Survey and others.

How stations were assigned

A station counts as a canal station when its coordinates fall inside a canal polygon, and it takes on that canal's attributes. Stations outside every polygon but within 500 m of one form the open-water comparison set, each matched to its nearest canal. Results run from January 2015 to October 2026. Units were standardized, rejected results dropped, and physically impossible values removed. Each station is summarized by its median, and the statistics use stations with at least 3 samples of a measure.

Network measures

Centerlines (the navigable middle of each canal) were joined end to end into connected networks. Each canal gets the total navigable length of its network, the number of junctions (where three or more channels meet), the number of dead-end tips, and kilometers of canal per mouth.

Statistics

Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests compare each canal with the open water beside it. Kruskal–Wallis tests compare canal types. Spearman rank correlations relate water quality to network measures. Regressions model each measure on canal form, network length, salinity and water management district, with standard errors grouped by canal network so connected canals aren't counted as independent. Nutrients, chlorophyll and bacteria are modeled on a log scale.

Limits

Monitoring programs choose where and when to sample, so stations aren't a random draw of canals, and Pinellas, Lee and Palm Beach counties contribute the most. Dissolved oxygen swings through the day and with depth; these results don't separate morning from afternoon or bottom from surface. Distance to mouth isn't included yet: the inventory records how many mouths each canal has but not where they are. Once mouth points exist, each station's along-channel distance to its nearest mouth can be added. Map and download geometry is simplified to about 5 m.

Reference values

Dissolved oxygen: 5 mg/L or more is healthy for fish, 2 to 5 stresses sensitive species, below 2 is dangerous (UF/IFAS Extension). Florida's marine standard is written as oxygen saturation (Rule 62-302.533, F.A.C.).
